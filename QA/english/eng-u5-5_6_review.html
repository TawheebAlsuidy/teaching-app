5.6 The mystery of the Mary Celeste
What is the mystery? Read the article and find out.
Lead box: Sailors coming back from long voyages used to tell stories of lost cities, strange animals and boiling oceans. Most of these strange stories have been explained; others have stayed mysteries. The true story of the Mary Celeste is perhaps the most famous unexplained mystery of the sea.
[Complete illustrated textbook article available offline in assets/ref/pb_p037.webp with painting assets/media/pb37_mary_celeste.webp; structured paragraph anchors below for WB 5.6 A–C:]
Para 1 (Discovery, Dec 5th 1872): Captain Morehouse of the Dei Gratia sailing across the Atlantic Ocean from New York to Gibraltar; good wind & excellent visibility (>=5 km). At ~9 o'clock a sailor sighted another ship on the horizon.
Para 2 (Closer observation): Two hours later Morehouse looked through his telescope; strange movement (turned whenever wind turned), no answer to signal flags, made out name 'Mary Celeste'; nobody on deck, masts, or at wheel — steering herself.
Para 3 (Boarding ~100 metres apart): Morehouse and two crew rowed across; Morehouse and one man climbed aboard, checked deck and every cabin — completely deserted.
Para 4 (Clues inside cabins — no panic): Crew cabins tidy; kitchen pans half-full of food over dead fire; captain's cabin had half-eaten meal on table & child's dress on sewing machine; gold, jewellery and money untouched in cupboard.
Para 5–6 (Two puzzling items & unsolved end): Blood-stained sword (made them wonder about mutiny, yet ship's boats still hung in place); two 2-metre pieces of wood cut out of bow rails on both sides. None of the people was ever seen again; deserted ship found drifting has never been explained.
